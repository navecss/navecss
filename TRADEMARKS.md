# Brand and name

The [MIT licence](./LICENSE) covers the **code**. It says nothing about names or logos, and that
silence is not permission. Permissive code licences grant rights in software, not in marks, which
is why projects that care about their identity publish a separate policy. This is ours.

**"Nave", "NaveCSS" and the Nave compass mark are trademarks of the NaveCSS project, and they are
not covered by the MIT licence.** They identify this project and where its code comes from. That is
the only job they have, and every rule below follows from it.

## Yes

- **Say your project is built with NaveCSS**, uses NaveCSS, or works with NaveCSS. That is true and
  useful, and it needs no permission from anyone.
- **Show the mark, unmodified, to say exactly that**: a "built with NaveCSS" badge in a README, a
  footer, a slide, a conference talk, a comparison post.
- **Use the name in prose**: documentation, blog posts, package descriptions, tutorials,
  comparisons, and criticism.
- **Scale the mark proportionally**, and re-colour it through the `--nave-logo-primary` and
  `--nave-logo-accent` custom properties the themeable SVG ships with. Re-colouring through those
  properties is a designed feature of the asset, not a modification of the mark.

Link back to the project where that is practical. Not a condition, just good manners.

## No

- **Do not modify the mark.** No redrawing, re-proportioning, rotating, recomposing, adding to it,
  or using a part of it on its own. A modified mark points at the wrong origin, which is the one
  thing a mark exists to prevent.
- **Do not use it as your own logo**, or as part of your own logo, and do not use a mark
  confusingly similar to it.
- **Do not imply endorsement.** Nothing you publish should suggest that NaveCSS endorses, sponsors,
  or is affiliated with you, your product, or your company, unless we have actually said so.

## The assets

The brand assets are **not shipped inside the published `@navecss/*` packages**. A package that
declares itself MIT while containing a reserved mark would contradict this policy, so the mark
stays out of the tarballs.

Their home is the brand page on the documentation site, which carries this policy on the same page
as the download, and they travel with their own licence note. Take them from there rather than
lifting them out of a rendered page: a screenshot crop of a logo helps nobody. While that page is
still being built, ask and we will send you the files.

## If you are unsure

Ask, before rather than after. This policy exists so the answer is obvious and nobody has to
guess; a use we would rather you changed is a conversation, not an accusation.
